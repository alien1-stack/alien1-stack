Phantom: First Hour

A new wallet’s first job is to receive. Its first screen should make that a single tap, not a trading pitch.

ScopeHome, Receive, OnboardingMethodFresh install, $0 balanceStatusIndependent concept
01 / PROBLEM

A fresh install, a $0 wallet, and a first screen that sells.

I installed the app today and watched what a brand-new user sees. Most people open a new wallet to do one thing: get something into it. The home screen has other plans.

1

The home screen opens like a casino.

With nothing in the wallet, the top of the screen says “Fund your account to start trading”, followed by a Trending list of memecoins already down 15 to 17 percent. Someone who only wants to be paid is shown speculation before they see their own wallet.

Seen first: a trade pitch.
Needed first: a way to receive.
2

The address is three taps deep.

Receiving is the most natural first action, yet an empty wallet has no Receive button. The address lives behind avatar, then account, then a small copy icon. A user who is waiting on a payment has to go looking for it.

Avatar → Account → tiny copy icon.
3

The @handle is a hidden superpower.

A username exists, but nothing says it can receive funds. There is no payment link and no QR to share, so people fall back to pasting a 44-character address and hoping nobody mistypes it.

A readable name, with no way to use it.
02 / REDESIGN

Let the user’s intent set the layout.

Three connected changes. Ask what they want to do first, then build the empty wallet around the answer, then make the most common action effortless.

Receive first.
Trade when there’s something to trade.

An empty wallet has one honest job. Everything that assumes funds, like markets and swaps, steps back until there are some, and stays one tap away for anyone who wants it.

Red markers on the Before screens point to the pain point with the same number. Before screens are simplified, neutral reconstructions.
A

Empty-wallet home

The old home leads with a funding prompt and a falling-token list. The new one leads with a card that does the job: your QR, your @handle, and a one-tap copy.

03 / WHY IT’S BETTER

Four tests, one answer each.

How this redesign holds up against the way I think a redesign should be judged.

Solves a real user problem

A brand-new user can get paid in one tap.

  • The most common first action, receiving, moves from three taps deep to the first thing on the screen.
  • The @handle becomes a usable payment identity instead of a label.
  • A request-amount link replaces “what’s your address?” and “how much, again?”.
Product thinking

Intent in, layout out.

  • One onboarding question personalises the first session without adding a settings maze.
  • Markets are collapsed, not removed, so the growth surface is deferred to when users can act on it.
  • Three metrics below say how I would find out if I am wrong.
Visual identity

Violet night, one loud signal.

  • Deep ink and violet keep the wallet’s dark, calm character.
  • A single signal lime marks the one action that matters on each screen.
  • Instrument Serif headlines with Geist for UI, and mono labels for anything an address or number touches.
Taste

Restraint over decoration.

  • Fewer things on the first screen, each with a reason. The empty state is quiet, not empty.
  • Consistent 24px margins, 20 to 28px radii and a short type scale across every screen.
  • Real copy throughout, including the small honest details like a dimmed Send.
04 / NEXT

What I’d test next.

Design is a hypothesis. These three numbers would tell me whether it earned its place.

Speed

Time to first address copy or share

From first launch to the first copy or share of an address or payment link, for new wallets.

Hypothesis: falls to under 10 seconds, because the action is on the first screen.
Activation

Funded within 24 hours

Share of new wallets that receive their first deposit inside a day, split by the onboarding choice.

Hypothesis: Receive users fund faster, since sharing a link is easier than reading out an address.
Adoption

@handle payment links shared

Share of new users who send a payment link or QR in their first hour, and how often it leads to a payment.

Hypothesis: the handle becomes the default way people ask for money.
Guardrail: Day-7 trade rate and Markets “Show” taps. Collapsing Markets should delay the discovery of trading, not remove it. If the day-7 numbers drop, the answer is a smarter default for the Explore group, not a return to the casino.
