**Phantom: First Hour. Redesigning the first screens of a brand-new wallet.**

I installed Phantom fresh and looked at what a $0 user sees. Most people open a new wallet to receive something. The app opens with "Fund your account to start trading" and a Trending list of memecoins already down 15 to 17 percent. The receive address sits three taps deep (avatar, account, a small copy icon), and the @handle gives no sign that it can be used to get paid.

**The core insight:** an empty wallet has one honest job, which is to receive. So the user's intent should set the layout.

**What I redesigned (3 screens):**
1. **Empty-wallet home.** A Receive card leads, with QR, @handle and one-tap copy. Markets collapse into a single quiet row until the first deposit. Send is dimmed.
2. **Receive sheet.** The @handle becomes a headline ("share it like an email address, for money"), with the QR, the full address, a one-tap "Share payment link" and an optional "Request amount" so the payer sees exactly what is asked.
3. **Onboarding last step.** "What do you want to do first? Receive, Buy or Explore" sets the home layout. It reorders the screen and never hides features.

The visual identity is violet night with one signal-lime action per screen, Instrument Serif headlines and Geist for UI. The case study ends with three metrics to test next.
